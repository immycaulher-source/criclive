Nepal Premier League Season 3 · 26 Oct – 21 Nov 2026 · Last updated 5 October 2026

The third season of the Nepal Premier League (NPL) is almost here. Eight franchises will play at the Tribhuvan University International Cricket Ground in Kirtipur, and fans everywhere want to know the same thing: where can I watch it live? This guide collects the official broadcast and streaming partners announced by the Cricket Association of Nepal (CAN), explains how to watch from Nepal, India and the rest of the world, and tells you what to check before the first ball.

Please note: broadcast details can change. Everything below is based on the rights announcement made by CAN on 7 September 2026 and reports that followed. Subscription prices and some regional partners had not been published when we wrote this, and we say so where that is the case. Always confirm with the official channels before match day.

Who owns the NPL 2026 broadcast rights?

CAN opened the bids for the media and ticketing rights of the third edition on 7 September 2026 and announced three winners:

  • Linear TV rights for Nepal: Himalaya Television Network Pvt. Ltd., for NPR 14.64 crore. Himalaya said its bid was more than five times last year's TV rights value.
  • OTT rights for the whole world: Netvadi Pvt. Ltd. (NetTV), for NPR 18.04 crore. The only other bidder was Digo Stream, the streaming arm backed by Dish Home.
  • Ticketing rights: eSewa Ltd., for NPR 85 lakh. Under the agreed terms CAN receives 95 percent of ticketing income.

Together the TV and OTT rights are worth more than Rs 32 crore, a big jump for a league that is only in its third season.

How to watch NPL 2026 on TV in Nepal

Himalaya Television, through its sports channel Himalaya Sports, is the official TV broadcaster for Nepal. How you receive it depends on your connection:

  • Cable, DTH and IPTV: ask your operator whether Himalaya Sports is included in your package or sold as an add-on. Operators often add a separate sports pack for big events.
  • Check the channel number early. Do this a few days before the 26 October opener so you are not searching during the first over.

For comparison, last year's season was shown on Kantipur Max (with Nepali commentary on Kantipur Max 2) alongside Star Sports. Those arrangements belonged to Season 2 and have not been announced for Season 3.

How to stream NPL 2026 online

NetTV is the official OTT partner. Because it holds the rights for the entire world, it is the first place to look for a legal live stream whether you are in Kathmandu, Doha or London.

What to do

  1. Install the official NetTV app on your phone, tablet or Android TV from the Play Store or App Store, and create an account.
  2. Watch for the NPL 2026 package announcement. NetTV had not published NPL pricing when we wrote this, so we cannot quote a price.
  3. Pay with the options the app offers, then open the match from the live section when the toss time approaches.

Tips for a smooth stream

  • Use a stable connection. A steady 4 to 5 Mbps is a reasonable target for HD video on one device.
  • Prefer Wi-Fi or fibre for the full 20 overs, and keep mobile data as a backup.
  • Update the app before match day, and sign in once in advance to avoid last-minute login problems.
  • Stay with official apps. Unofficial streams are unreliable, often illegal, and can be unsafe for your device.

Watching NPL 2026 from India

No Indian television broadcaster had been announced when this page was last updated. For reference, Season 2 (2025) was shown on Star Sports Select 2 and streamed on FanCode, but that does not mean the same deal applies this year. Since NetTV holds worldwide OTT rights, its app is the most likely official option, but availability in India should be confirmed before the tournament. We will update this page as soon as a broadcaster is announced.

Watching NPL 2026 abroad

For viewers in the UK, Australia, the Gulf, the USA and elsewhere, NetTV's worldwide OTT rights mean there should be an official stream. Regional restrictions can still apply, so check the NetTV app or website in your country. Avoid workarounds that break a platform's terms of use; the safest route is always the official partner.
